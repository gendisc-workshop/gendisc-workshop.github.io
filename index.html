Workshop scope

Generative models are enabling protein and enzyme design, antimicrobial peptide discovery, materials synthesis, and astronomical reconstruction from sparse measurements. GenDisc studies the principles that turn candidate generation into scientific discovery: exploring beyond known data, adapting to experimental feedback, and selecting informative experiments.

GenDisc brings together generative modelling, sequential decision-making, optimisation, and the experimental sciences around four shared challenges:

  • Objectives. Formalising novelty, diversity, feasibility, and scientific value, including rare outcomes and competing goals.
  • Exploration. Reaching valid, valuable candidates beyond the regions covered by training data.
  • Learning from feedback. Adapting generators and choosing experiments under scarce data, uncertain measurements, and limited budgets.
  • Validation. Connecting computational metrics to experimental outcomes and distinguishing novelty to a model from novelty to science.

Our aim is to establish shared objectives, algorithms, and standards of evidence. Scientific applications expose assumptions and limitations, helping shape foundations and methods that transfer across biology, chemistry, neuroscience, ecology, physics, and beyond.

Call for papers

Draft call · submissions not open

We welcome work on generative methods for discovering scientifically valuable structures, designs, and hypotheses. Contributions should offer insights into generative discovery that may extend beyond a single application. Perspectives, benchmarks, and negative results are welcome in every format.

Topics of interest

Theory and foundations

  • Novelty, diversity, scientific value, and generative reach.
  • Rare-event, risk-sensitive, and multi-objective formulations.
  • Exploration and coverage beyond the training distribution.
  • Optimisation theory and the sample, computational, and experimental complexity of discovery.

Methods and systems

  • Generative optimisation and inverse design.
  • Steering, composing, and fine-tuning generators for exploration and discovery.
  • Adaptation from experimental or multi-fidelity feedback; simulation-to-experiment transfer.
  • Active experiment selection and closed-loop discovery under limited budgets.

Computational and experimental validation

  • Prospective experiments and high-fidelity validation of candidates and hypotheses.
  • Benchmarks for novelty, diversity, validity, utility, and efficiency.
  • Evaluation under noisy measurements, imperfect oracles, and feasibility constraints.
  • Reproducibility, robustness, and informative positive or negative results.

The topics are illustrative. All generative approaches are welcome, including diffusion and flow models, autoregressive and discrete models, GFlowNets, energy-based and latent-variable models, and evolutionary or hybrid search.

Outside the scope: pure prediction; generation assessed only by sample quality; general-purpose generative modelling or post-training without a discovery objective; and application-specific results without broader methodological insights.

Review and presentation

  • Double-blind, non-archival review on OpenReview, with at least two reviews per submission. We prioritise ongoing and novel work.
  • All accepted papers will be presented as posters; selected papers will receive contributed talks.
  • Work under review or subsequently published elsewhere is welcome, subject to the other venues’ policies.

Under the proposed guidelines, short and tiny papers must be primarily human-authored. AI assistance is permitted; AI-generated papers are not.

Panel

Bridging the Gaps in Generative Discovery

A proposed 60-minute panel on two gaps that shape generative discovery.

Module 01

Bridging theory and experiment

Relating theoretical objectives and algorithmic advances to experimental needs and constraints. The discussion covers model quality, optimisation, experiment selection, and the evidence needed to establish scientific progress.

Module 02

Bridging scientific domains

Identifying methods that transfer across sciences with different assumptions and priorities. The discussion covers simulation and experimental feedback, alongside shared meanings of diversity, novelty, and discovery.
