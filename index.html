01 / The idea

From generating candidates
to making discoveries.

Generative models are opening new avenues for discovering molecules, materials, proteins, devices, and scientific hypotheses. Real discovery connects these models to objectives, algorithms, and experiments.

GenDisc brings together researchers in generative modelling, sequential decision-making, optimisation, and the experimental sciences. We ask how practical scientific constraints should shape the way models explore, adapt, and learn from feedback.

Our aim is to establish shared principles for formulating discovery objectives, designing algorithms, and evaluating evidence—principles that transfer across biology, chemistry, neuroscience, ecology, physics, and beyond.

02 / Research themes

A shared language
for discovery.

Three complementary perspectives connect mathematical foundations to scalable methods and experimental evidence.

I / FORMULATE
◎

Theory &
foundations

What should a discovery system optimise, and what guarantees can we give?

  • Novelty, diversity, and scientific value
  • Rare events and competing objectives
  • Exploration beyond the training distribution
  • Limits on the complexity of discovery
II / DESIGN
↗

Methods &
systems

How do we direct and adapt generators using scarce, costly, or uncertain feedback?

  • Generative optimisation and inverse design
  • Steering and fine-tuning generative models
  • Active and cost-aware experiment selection
  • Closed-loop discovery and multi-fidelity learning
III / VALIDATE
✳

Computational &
experimental validation

What evidence establishes that a generative discovery method has succeeded?

  • Prospective experimental validation
  • Benchmarks for novelty, validity, and utility
  • Robustness under imperfect measurements
  • Reproducibility and informative negative results

All generative approaches are welcome. Contributions should offer insights that can extend beyond a single scientific application.

04 / The panel

Bridging the Gaps in Generative Discovery

A proposed 60-minute discussion connecting theoretical principles, experimental practice, and perspectives across the sciences.

Module 01

Bridging theory and experiment

Connecting theoretical formulations and algorithmic advances with the needs and constraints of experimental science. This module explores the roles of model quality, optimisation, experimental design, and experimental validation in turning generative methods into scientific progress.

Module 02

Bridging scientific domains

Identifying shared principles across sciences with different assumptions, tools, and priorities. This module examines the transfer of methods between domains, the role of simulation and experimental feedback, and common ground in the meanings of diversity, novelty, and discovery.

05 / Contribute

Bring your open questions.
And your new discoveries.

Submissions not yet open

We plan to welcome theory, methods, systems, and scientific validation, alongside perspectives, benchmarks, and negative results.

The proposed review process is double-blind and non-archival, hosted on OpenReview. The final call for papers and submission link will be published following workshop acceptance.

9 pagesFull papers
4 pagesShort papers
2 pagesTiny papers

Proposed maximum lengths, excluding references and appendices. Submission requirements remain provisional.

What makes a contribution a good fit?

Work should address a discovery-related objective and offer broader methodological insights. We welcome different generative model families, including diffusion and flow models, autoregressive models, GFlowNets, energy-based models, and evolutionary or hybrid approaches.

Pure prediction, unconditional generation assessed only by sample quality, and application-specific results without transferable discovery insights fall outside the proposed scope.
